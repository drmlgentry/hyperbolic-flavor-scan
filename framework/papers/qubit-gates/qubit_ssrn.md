## SSRN Abstract — Qubit Gates Paper

**Title:** Topologically Protected Qubit Gate Configurations from
Hyperbolic 3-Manifold Holonomy

**Abstract:**

Every element of the holonomy representation
ρ: π₁(M) → PSL(2,C) of a compact hyperbolic 3-manifold acts on C²
as a rotation of the Bloch sphere, defining a natural qubit gate.
We study the mathematical structure of gate configurations arising
from the manifold m003 (OrientableClosedCensus[1], H₁ = Z/5,
vol = 0.981), whose fundamental group encodes the lepton mixing
matrix of the Standard Model in the Hyperbolic Flavor Geometry
framework.

We prove two structural theorems. The Covering Tower Invariance
Theorem establishes that the optimal gate triple — three words in
π₁(m003) whose Borel lower-triangular construction reproduces the
absolute PMNS lepton mixing matrix with Frobenius fitness 0.01897 —
yields identical mixing matrices when lifted to the degree-2 and
degree-3 covers of m003 preserving Z/5 torsion, with maximum element
deviation below 10⁻⁴. The gate configuration is therefore a
topological invariant of the Z/5-preserving covering tower, not of
any particular word representative.

The Homology Class No-Go Theorem establishes that any gate triple
containing two words with identical image in H₁(M) = Z/p forces the
Jarlskog CP-violation invariant J = 0 identically, providing a
topological obstruction to CP-violating quantum operations. The
obstruction arises because homology class collision forces one column
of the resulting unitary matrix to be real, which is sufficient to
kill J. The quark sector manifold m006(-5,2) realizes exactly this
obstruction: its optimal gate triple has two words with identical
homology class, yielding J = 0 at leading order (observed J ≈ 0.031
is a higher-order correction).

These results establish a new connection between the algebraic
topology of hyperbolic 3-manifolds (covering towers, abelianization
of the fundamental group) and quantum information theory (gate
universality, CP-violating operations, resource theory of homology
class diversity). All results are computed from SnapPy census data
and are fully reproducible.
